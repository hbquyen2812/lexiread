# Grammar content review

Reviewed: 2026-10-26. Scope: `data/grammar_lessons.json` only; UI and scoring are integrated separately.

## Inventory

- Version 1; 30 lessons, five each at A1, A2, B1, B2, C1 and C2.
- Six original four-option questions per lesson: 180 questions total.
- Two explanation cards and two pitfalls per lesson: 60 of each.
- Each explanation has an English example and a natural Vietnamese translation.
- Every question has a single keyed answer and Vietnamese reasoning.
- Prerequisites refer only to lessons that appear earlier; no cycles or missing references.
- Topics use general, TOEIC and/or IELTS tags to indicate useful practice contexts. They are not official exam questions.

The levels are an editorial learning sequence, not an official rule-to-CEFR mapping, placement test, complete CEFR syllabus or certification. Several structures occur below C2 in primary references; the C2 practice revisits them through focus, meaning, register and precision. This belongs in one concise provenance note, rather than repeated labels beside every lesson.

## Ambiguity and language review

All 180 questions were reviewed with their complete option set. The review specifically checked that:

- Who and that, or may and might, are not presented as competing answers where both fit.
- Will, going to and the present continuous are not treated as mutually exclusive in all future contexts.
- Reported-speech questions explicitly request backshift where it is needed; the explanation allows a present form when a statement remains true.
- Past-perfect meaning questions specify the earlier event. Simply being a long time ago does not require past perfect.
- Deduction questions specify the requested degree of certainty when several modal verbs would be grammatical.
- Participle-clause questions retain the intended subject; passive forms are distinguished from active forms.
- Fronting questions distinguish object fronting from negative inversion and identify the natural register when pronouns are involved.
- Nominalisation preserves the actor, uncertainty and chronology; it does not turn sequence into proof of causation.
- A1-be q6 originally had a potentially grammatical distractor, “The cupboard does not empty.” It was replaced with a structurally invalid option.
- B1-relative-clauses q6 now specifies that the woman made the call, avoiding confusion with a woman called by someone else.
- C2-ellipsis q1 specifies retaining the question's auxiliary; q2 specifies ability, avoiding an alternative habitual reading with does.
- Vietnamese instructions explain the reason for the answer in plain language, without referring to implementation details.

The questions mix sentence completion, selecting a correctly formed sentence, interpreting meaning and choosing a rewrite that preserves the original information. The advanced set deliberately includes context and register, rather than only obscure forms.
